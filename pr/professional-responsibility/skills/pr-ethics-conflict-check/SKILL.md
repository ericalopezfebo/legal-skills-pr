# Puerto Rico Professional Responsibility Conflict Check

## Governing authority

- **Primary and controlling authority:** Reglas de Conducta Profesional de Puerto Rico, approved by the Supreme Court of Puerto Rico in *In re Reglas de Conducta Profesional de Puerto Rico*, Resolución ER-2025-02 (June 17, 2025), as corrected by the official errata, effective January 1, 2026.
- **Do not analyze current Puerto Rico professional-responsibility questions under the former Cánones de Ética Profesional.** The former 1970 code was superseded when the new Rules became effective.
- **ABA Model Rules of Professional Conduct:** may be used as persuasive/reference material, especially where the Puerto Rico Rules or comments expressly make them useful as a reference. They are not controlling Puerto Rico law and must not be substituted for the Puerto Rico text.
- When Puerto Rico text and an ABA Model Rule differ, **Puerto Rico controls**.
- For notarial matters, also identify potentially applicable Puerto Rico notarial statutes, regulations, jurisprudence, and ODIN guidance.

## Core rules for this skill

Begin with the following Puerto Rico Rules and expand as the facts require:

- **Rule 1.0** — Definitions.
- **Rule 1.6** — Confidentiality of information.
- **Rule 1.7** — Duties to current clients / concurrent conflicts.
- **Rule 1.8** — Current-client conflicts: specific rules.
- **Rule 1.9** — Duties to former clients.
- **Rule 1.10** — Imputation of conflicts; screening/isolation in specified circumstances.
- **Rule 1.11** — Special conflicts for former/current government officers and employees.
- **Rule 1.12** — Former judge, arbitrator, mediator, or other neutral.
- **Rule 1.13** — Organization as client.
- **Rule 1.16** — Declining or terminating representation.
- **Rule 1.18** — Duties to prospective clients.
- **Rules 7.1–7.3** — Communications concerning legal services and solicitation.
- **Rule 8.4** — Misconduct where deception, dishonesty, improper influence, or related conduct is implicated.

Relevant ABA analogues commonly include Model Rules 1.6–1.12, 1.18, 7.1–7.3, and 8.4. Cite them only as ABA Model Rules, never as Puerto Rico authority.

## When to apply

- Screening a proposed client or matter against current clients, former clients, prospective clients, related parties, or personal interests.
- Determining whether a conflict is personal or imputable to the law office and whether screening/isolation is available.
- Reviewing whether confidential information may be used or disclosed.
- Reviewing intake communications for duties owed to prospective clients.
- Reviewing attorney advertising, websites, social-media posts, referral arrangements, direct outreach, or solicitation.
- Reviewing whether withdrawal or rejection of a representation may be required because of a conflict.
- **Out of scope:** this skill is a screening and issue-spotting aid. It does not replace a law office conflicts database, attorney judgment, controlling authority, or a formal ethics opinion where needed.

## Algorithm

1. **Establish the governing date and jurisdiction.**
   - For conduct on or after January 1, 2026, analyze under the Reglas de Conducta Profesional de Puerto Rico.
   - If conduct predates January 1, 2026, flag the temporal issue and determine whether the former ethical code governs that historical conduct instead of silently applying the new Rules retroactively.
   - Confirm whether another jurisdiction's rules may also apply under Rule 8.5 or another choice-of-law provision.

2. **Gather only information reasonably necessary for the conflict screen.**
   - Identify the prospective/current client, adverse parties, relevant affiliates, matter description, opposing counsel when relevant, current/former clients, prior firms or government service, and personal relationships or interests that may materially limit the representation.
   - For an initial prospective-client inquiry, avoid eliciting unnecessary confidential or potentially disqualifying details.

3. **Screen current-client conflicts under Rule 1.7.**
   Ask whether:
   - the proposed representation is directly adverse to another current client;
   - there is a significant risk that representation will be substantially limited by duties to another current client, former client, third person, or the lawyer's personal interests; or
   - the relationship described in Rule 1.7(a)(3) exists between the lawyer and counsel for an adverse party.

   If a concurrent conflict exists, do **not** assume consent cures it. Determine whether every Rule 1.7(b) condition is satisfied, including reasonable belief in competent and diligent representation, absence of a legal prohibition, no prohibited claim between jointly represented clients, and informed consent from each affected client **ratified in writing**.

4. **Screen specific conflicts under Rule 1.8.**
   - Check the specific transaction, compensation, gift, aggregate-settlement, financial-assistance, sexual-relationship, proprietary-interest, third-party-payment, and other limitations that may apply.
   - Do not reduce a Rule 1.8 issue to only the general Rule 1.7 test if a specific provision governs.

5. **Screen former-client conflicts under Rule 1.9.**
   - Determine whether the new matter is the same or substantially related and whether the new client's interests are substantially adverse to the former client's interests.
   - Where Rule 1.9 permits waiver, require the former client's informed consent **ratified in writing**.
   - Independently check Rule 1.9(c) restrictions on use or disclosure of information relating to the former representation.

6. **Screen imputation and mobility issues under Rules 1.10–1.12.**
   - Determine whether an individual lawyer's conflict is imputed to the law office.
   - Do not state that every conflict is automatically firm-wide. Apply the actual exceptions and screening/isolation mechanisms in the governing Puerto Rico Rule.
   - For qualifying former-firm conflicts under Rule 1.10(a)(2), examine timely isolation, fee restrictions, prompt written notice to the affected former client, and required certifications.
   - For former/current government lawyers, judges, arbitrators, mediators, or neutrals, apply Rules 1.11 or 1.12.

7. **Check prospective-client duties under Rule 1.18.**
   - Determine whether the interaction created a prospective-client relationship under the circumstances.
   - Even without a formal engagement, information obtained from a prospective client generally may not be used or revealed except as Rule 1.18 permits through Rule 1.9 principles.
   - If the lawyer received information that could be significantly harmful in the same or a substantially related matter, assess disqualification and imputation.
   - Where Rule 1.18(d) applies, evaluate informed consent ratified in writing or the permitted screening/isolation route, including prompt written notice.

8. **Check confidentiality under Rule 1.6 separately from privilege.**
   - Flag any use or disclosure of information relating to representation unless informed consent, implied authorization, or a specific Rule 1.6(b) exception applies.
   - Do not describe Rule 1.6 confidentiality as identical to attorney-client privilege.
   - For conflict detection arising from lawyer mobility or changes in law-office composition/ownership, consider the limited disclosure permitted by Rule 1.6(b)(7), including its protections against prejudice and loss of privilege.

9. **If reviewing advertising or solicitation, apply Rules 7.1–7.3 as written.**
   - **Rule 7.1:** no false or misleading communications, material misrepresentations or omissions, or statements likely to create unjustified expectations without a reasonable factual basis.
   - **Rule 7.2:** check permissible media, payments for recommendations/referrals, reciprocal-referral arrangements, specialization/expert claims, and required responsible-lawyer/contact information.
   - **Rule 7.3:** determine whether the communication is a solicitation directed to a specific person known or reasonably known to need legal services in a particular matter; check restrictions on live person-to-person solicitation for pecuniary gain and the prohibitions on coercion, coaction/pressure, harassment, or continuing after a person declines solicitation.
   - Do **not** rely on descriptions of what was historically restricted under the former Cánones when the current Rules supply the governing test.

10. **Use ABA Model Rules only as secondary reference.**
    - If a Puerto Rico Rule or comment leaves interpretive uncertainty, compare the analogous ABA Model Rule and comments.
    - Label the comparison expressly as persuasive/reference material.
    - Never state that an ABA Model Rule governs Puerto Rico unless independently required by a valid choice-of-law analysis.

11. **Classify the result with precision.**
    Use one of these conclusions:
    - **No conflict or ethics issue identified on the facts provided.**
    - **Potential issue — additional facts required.**
    - **Conflict exists but may be consentable if every condition of the applicable Rule is satisfied.**
    - **Conflict appears nonconsentable / representation should be declined or terminated, subject to attorney review and tribunal requirements.**
    - **Communication should be revised before use.**

    Do not describe a conflict as "cleared" merely because the user has not supplied facts showing a violation.

## Citation and accuracy rules

- Cite **specific Puerto Rico Rule numbers** when the text has been verified. Do not use "cánon aplicable" for current conduct governed by the 2025 Rules.
- Distinguish clearly among **Puerto Rico controlling authority**, **ABA Model Rules as persuasive/reference material**, statutes/regulations, and factual assumptions.
- If quoting or paraphrasing a rule, verify the current Puerto Rico text, including official errata.
- Do not invent a waiver, screening mechanism, or exception merely because a similar ABA provision exists.
- Do not say all concurrent conflicts are consentable; Rule 1.7(b) imposes independent conditions.
- Do not say all conflicts are automatically imputed; Rules 1.10–1.12 contain distinctions and exceptions.
- Do not say all direct solicitation is prohibited; apply Rule 7.3's current definitions, exceptions, and restrictions.
- When conduct occurred before January 1, 2026, flag the effective-date issue before choosing the governing ethical code.
- This skill identifies issues and applicable rules; the attorney responsible for the matter makes the final professional judgment.
