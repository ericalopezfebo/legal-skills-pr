# Risk-sensitive clauses

The following provisions require current-law verification before drafting them as enforceable:

## Non-compete / non-solicitation
Check the current Puerto Rico rule, the context of the relationship, duration, geographic or activity scope, consideration, specificity, public policy, and any federal overlay. Do not copy a mainland-U.S. clause.

## Assignment / cession
Determine what is being assigned: rights, obligations, the entire contractual position, or another interest. Check consent requirements and effects against third parties.

## Option clauses
Identify the object, exercise mechanics, term, consideration if relevant, and whether the provision is truly an option or another preliminary arrangement. Verify current TSPR authority.

## Adhesion provisions
Flag pre-drafted terms, bargaining asymmetry, ambiguous provisions and terms that may implicate consumer or public-policy rules.

## Penalty / liquidated damages
Distinguish agreed remedies from ordinary damages and verify the current Civil Code treatment.

## Government contracts
Do not assume a generic clause set. Identify the contracting entity, source of funds, procurement regime and any mandatory statutory/regulatory clauses.

## Employment-related restrictions
Screen for Puerto Rico labor law and federal preemption/overlay before relying on Civil Code freedom-of-contract principles.

## Solar/consumer and other regulated agreements
Check special statutes, regulations, cancellation rights and mandatory disclosures.
